# PBS-PRESS-RELEASE.md  
## Pale Blue Systems Foundation Launches Open Communication Standard for the Next Era of Space Exploration

**For Immediate Release**

**January 2026**

---

### A New Foundation for How Space Systems Communicate

The **Pale Blue Systems Foundation (PBSF)** today announced the public release of the **Pale Blue Systems Open Standard (PBS)**—a new, open communication framework designed to support reliable, interoperable data exchange across space, lunar, planetary, and other extreme environments.

As humanity returns to the Moon, expands commercial activity in orbit, and prepares for future missions to Mars, communication systems are becoming more distributed, more international, and more complex. PBS is designed to help those systems work together safely and predictably, even when connections are delayed, disrupted, or unavailable for long periods of time.

---

### Why This Matters

On Earth, most digital communication assumes constant connectivity and near-instant response. Space does not work that way.

Space systems must operate across:
- long and variable communication delays  
- intermittent or scheduled contact windows  
- harsh and unpredictable environments  
- multiple organizations operating independently  

These realities make it difficult for spacecraft, rovers, habitats, and ground teams to share information reliably—especially when missions involve multiple agencies or commercial partners.

The Pale Blue Systems Open Standard addresses this challenge by providing a **shared communication language** that allows systems to interoperate without requiring shared vendors, shared hardware, or proprietary disclosure.

---

### Aligned with NASA and International Space Goals

The Foundation’s work aligns with critical challenges identified by NASA and other space agencies.

NASA’s Space Technology Mission Directorate has formally identified communications, networking, and coordination as key technology shortfalls that must be addressed to support sustained lunar presence, cislunar infrastructure, and future Mars exploration. These challenges include the need for systems that can operate autonomously, tolerate long delays, and continue functioning when connections are disrupted.

Relevant NASA documents include:

- **NASA 2026 Civil Space Shortfall Ranking**  
  https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/space-technology-roadmaps/2026-civil-space-shortfall-ranking/

- **NASA Moon to Mars Architecture Definition Document**  
  https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/moon-to-mars-architecture/

PBS is designed to complement existing space architectures—such as NASA’s Delay/Disruption Tolerant Networking (DTN)—by making it easier for diverse systems to communicate across organizational and technical boundaries.

---

### What the PBS Open Standard Does

At its core, PBS defines **how messages are packaged, identified, prioritized, and safely forwarded** in environments where traditional internet assumptions do not apply.

In practical terms, the standard enables:
- spacecraft and rovers to exchange data even when direct connections are unavailable  
- systems from different organizations to interoperate without custom integration  
- safety-critical information to be prioritized and delivered reliably  
- future missions to scale without locking participants into a single vendor  

PBS does not replace mission software or communication hardware. Instead, it acts as a **middleware layer**, translating between applications and the underlying radios, lasers, relays, and ground stations that carry data through space.

---

### Open Standards, Neutral Stewardship

The Pale Blue Systems Foundation was established to steward the PBS Open Standard as a **neutral, open, and durable public resource**.

Key principles include:
- open access to specifications  
- transparent governance and review  
- long-term stability and backward compatibility  
- separation between standards stewardship and commercial products  

This approach mirrors successful models that helped the internet grow—where shared standards enabled innovation, competition, and global collaboration.

Commercial companies, research institutions, and space agencies can all build on PBS while retaining the freedom to innovate internally.

---

### Supporting a Growing Space Economy

As commercial activity expands beyond Earth, interoperability becomes essential. No single organization will own or operate all of the systems involved in future exploration and development.

By lowering integration barriers and reducing engineering risk, PBS helps:
- commercial companies participate in multi-party missions  
- governments and agencies collaborate across borders  
- educators and researchers build systems aligned with real mission needs  

The result is a communication foundation designed not just for individual missions, but for a shared and sustainable space future.

---

### About the Pale Blue Systems Foundation

The Pale Blue Systems Foundation is an independent organization chartered to steward open standards and reference frameworks for communication in space and other extreme environments.

Operating with a public-benefit mission, the Foundation exists to ensure that the technical language used to exchange information beyond Earth remains open, interoperable, and accessible to all who explore, study, and work in space.

---

### Looking Ahead

The release of the PBS Open Standard marks an important step toward building shared infrastructure for humanity’s expansion beyond Earth.

By focusing on interoperability, resilience, and neutrality, the Pale Blue Systems Foundation aims to help ensure that future space systems—whether civil, commercial, or international—can communicate, coordinate, and operate together safely.

For more information, visit the Pale Blue Systems Foundation repository and documentation.
